My self chat is full of thoughts I meant to revisit. My X bookmarks are full of things I meant to read. My voice notes are full of tiny details I was afraid I would forget.

I kept calling that a second brain. It behaved more like an attic: packed with interesting things, no clear path through it, and a growing suspicion I was saving objects for a version of me who never arrived.

The problem was not that I needed a better filing system. I wanted to know why these particular things had caught my attention — and whether the thread between them could become something I might actually make.

01The harvest

Three saves.
One subconscious brief.

Signal Garden is a private creative practice for cross-pollinating saved fragments into a small project, an essay seed or a post I choose to publish. It starts with a question that feels different from “what did I save?”

What am I paying attention to without noticing?

The demo plants three fictional fragments: a note about seeing the city before it asks anything of you; a saved line about the people who fix, paint, plant and sell; and a voice note about twenty-six photos of hand-painted signs with no names.

W Self chat · SG-01

“I want to see the city before it begins demanding things from me.”

X X bookmark · SG-02

“A city is edited every day by the people who fix, paint, plant and sell at its edges.”

V Voice note · SG-03

“I have twenty-six photos of hand-painted shop signs and none of them have names.”

Taken separately, these are a mood, an observation and a half-finished collection. Connected, they suggest The Unofficial City Index: a field guide to the quiet hands that make a neighbourhood feel alive.

The project idea is a leap. The fragments are evidence. Signal Garden keeps the two visibly separate.

A rule for the room

Every creative leap carries the source IDs that sparked it. No free-floating “insight.”

02The Switch room

Not one oracle.
A room with different jobs.

I wanted the agents to disagree productively and leave their working visible. So Signal Garden gives them separate jobs in one Switch room: evidence first, creative leap second, editorial pressure test last.

01A

Archivist

Finds motifs in the selected fragments. Quotes exact phrases, attaches source IDs, and labels interpretation as interpretation.

Evidence map
02M

Mycelium

Makes one surprising connection across moments. Proposes a vivid project someone can begin in under an hour.

A first move
03E

Editor

Challenges cliché, invented context and unsupported claims. Returns an optional post with the sources behind it.

A human choice

Switch is the workbench here. The person and each agent share a room, instructions and handoffs; the creative process stays inspectable instead of collapsing into a single answer. The included template creates the three agents and the garden-cross-pollination room.

In this first cut, I deliberately pass a selected source bundle into the room instead of asking agents to log into my personal accounts. The extra copy-and-paste is visible friction. The tradeoff is that no agent gets a back door into my messages or bookmarks.

The example outcome in the app uses fictional signals. The included sample room dialogue is labeled illustrative; a real demo recording should show the agents running in Switch.

03The publishing gate

A draft is a suggestion.
My name is still on it.

An agent can offer a sentence. It cannot decide that the sentence sounds like me, that the idea is ready, or that I want to post today.

The dispatch desk keeps the candidate editable and counts its characters. I have to mark it approved before the prefilled X composer opens. The final “post” action still happens in X, with me looking at the copy.

Nothing in the prototype signs into X, schedules a post or publishes in the background. Good personal agents should give me more agency over my attention, not quietly take over my voice.

04Build notes

A small tool.
A real Switch workflow.

The app is plain HTML, CSS and browser JavaScript. A tiny Node server serves it locally; there is no package install or build step. Captures and imports stay in browser storage. Each import is capped at 250 fragments to fit typical browser storage.

WhatsApp self-chat exports come in as text. X bookmark archives come in as JSON. Voice notes can be added as a transcript and a little context. There is no account login, analytics, backend or external AI call inside the app.

Try the projectLocal setup
  1. Start Switch Console, connect a chat bridge and sign in to a supported agent provider.
  2. Import the included switch/garden-room.template.yaml and create its room and agents.
  3. Run the app with npm start and open http://localhost:4173.
  4. Choose a few fragments, prepare the brief, and paste it into garden-cross-pollination.
  5. Follow the evidence → idea → edit handoffs. Bring the post back, approve it, and choose whether to publish.

The first version is intentionally modest about automation. Switch does the collaborative thinking in its room; Signal Garden holds the source fragments and the publishing decision. That seam is visible, so the owner can decide exactly what crosses it.
